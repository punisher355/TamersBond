# **Chapter 13: Digivolution**

Digivolution is not a button you press. It is a question your partner asks of you: do you have enough left? Every evolution is paid for in Hope, and Hope is not infinite.

## **Stages**

A Digimon moves through several distinct stages over the course of its life, and as a general rule, the higher the stage, the stronger the Digimon standing in it. Every stage brings a real jump in what a Digimon can do, not just a bigger number on the sheet.

| **Stage** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh | No stats, no moves. Narrative only. |
| In-Training | All base stats = 1. PR 1 signature move only. |
| Rookie | Starting stage for all new partners. PR 2–4. |
| Champion | First major digivolution milestone. PR 5–7. |
| Ultimate | Mid-campaign goal. PR 8–10. |
| Mega | Campaign endgame target. PR 10–12. Some Mega Digimon can digivolve into another Mega form (PR 13–15). |

## **Hope, Digivolution Fuel**

Hope is what fuels every digivolution and keeps a Digimon holding a stage above Default. Chapter 6 covers exactly how your Hope Pool is sized, off your total EXP earned over your whole career, not your current stats or any single Crest Stat's rank. What matters here is what that Hope actually gets spent on once you have it.

Hope fully restores whenever you rest.

| **Stage** | **Hope Cost Per Turn** |
| --- | --- |
| In-Training | 1 |
| Rookie | 3 |
| Champion | 6 |
| Ultimate | 10 |
| Mega | 15 |

| **Stacking Costs** When digivolved multiple stages above Default Stage, pay the SUM of all stage costs above Default. Example: Default = Rookie, Current = Ultimate, Cost = Champion (6) + Ultimate (10) = 16 Hope per Tamer turn |
| --- |

## **Default Stage, Current Stage, and Max Default Stage**

Three different terms describe where a Digimon actually stands at any given moment, and it's worth being clear on all three before getting into how digivolving actually works turn to turn.

Default Stage is where a Digimon rests when nothing is pushing them higher or lower. For a brand new partner, that's Rookie.

Current Stage is simply wherever the Digimon actually sits right now, this exact moment. It can match their Default Stage, or sit higher or lower than it, depending on what's happened so far this encounter.

Max Default Stage is the highest a Digimon's Default Stage is ever allowed to climb to over the course of a campaign. By default, that ceiling is Ultimate. A Digimon's Default Stage can rise through Daily Rest Recovery (covered further down this chapter) all the way up to Ultimate on its own, but pushing that ceiling any higher, into Mega, takes a class ability or a story-driven exception.

None of this changes the separate rule that a Digimon can digivolve up to 2 stages above whatever their Default Stage happens to be at any given time. That ceiling moves with your Default Stage; it isn't locked to any one absolute stage. With a Default Stage of Rookie, the highest reachable stage is Ultimate, 2 stages up. If a Digimon's Default Stage ever climbs to Champion instead, whether through Daily Rest Recovery, a class ability, or a story reason, the highest reachable stage automatically becomes Mega, 2 stages up from there.

The ceiling is always measured from Default Stage, never from wherever a Digimon's Current Stage happens to sit in the moment. Say a Digimon's Default Stage is Rookie, and their Tamer dedigivolved them down to In-Training earlier this fight. From In-Training, they can still digivolve all the way up to Ultimate, three steps up from where they're currently sitting, because the ceiling is still counting from Default Stage (Rookie), not from Current Stage (In-Training).

## **Digivolving**

Digivolving moves a Digimon one stage higher. It uses the Tamer's Digivolving Action on their own turn, and it can only be used once per round. You can only climb one stage at a time unless a class ability specifically says otherwise, and you can never push a Digimon's Current Stage more than 2 stages above their Default Stage.

A Digimon can only digivolve into a form its current form actually has a valid path to. Simply having a form unlocked somewhere in your pool isn't enough. If a Rookie has two different Champion forms unlocked but only one of them is reachable from its current species line, that's the only one available to digivolve into right now.

Digivolving to a higher stage also increases the Hope cost you pay each turn to hold that stage, since the Hope Cost Per Turn table above only climbs as the stage does.

## **Dedigivolving**

Dedigivolving drops a Digimon one or two stages lower. It uses a Move Action, either the Tamer's or the Digimon's own, though only one of the two can use theirs in a given round.

Dedigivolving isn't simply the reverse of digivolving into whatever lower-stage form you like. A Digimon can only dedigivolve back into the specific form it actually used to reach its current stage, not just any unlocked form that happens to sit at that lower stage. Several different In-Training Digimon might all be able to digivolve into Agumon, but if this particular Tamer's partner reached Agumon by digivolving from Koromon specifically, dedigivolving only ever takes them back to Koromon. Even if Budmon is also unlocked and also leads to Agumon on paper, it was never actually the form this Digimon used to get there, so it isn't available to dedigivolve into.

Dedigivolving to a lower stage reduces the Hope cost you pay each turn to maintain it, for the same reason in reverse.

## **Sidedigivolving**

Sidedigivolving swaps a Digimon into a different unlocked form at the same stage they're currently at, without moving up or down at all. It uses the Tamer's Digivolving Action, the same as digivolving up a stage.

A Digimon can only sidedigivolve into a form that shares the same previous form as the one they're currently in, and that form has to already be unlocked and gained by the Digimon. Koromon can digivolve into both Betamon and Agumon. If this Tamer's partner is currently Agumon, having come from Koromon, they can sidedigivolve over to Betamon instead, since Betamon also digivolves from that same Koromon. What that partner can't do is sidedigivolve into some other Rookie form that didn't come from Koromon, even if it happens to be unlocked somewhere else in the pool.

Since Sidedigivolving never changes what stage a Digimon is at, it doesn't change the Hope cost paid each turn either.

| **Status Effects and Stage Changes** All active status effects clear immediately whenever a Digimon changes stage or form this way, whether that's digivolving, dedigivolving, or sidedigivolving, voluntary or forced. |
| --- |

## **Forcing a Digivolution**

When a Tamer digivolves their partner, they choose how much Hope to spend to sustain that stage: anywhere from the full Hope Cost Per Turn required, down to 0. Paying the full amount keeps the digivolution completely stable from that point on. Paying anything less is forcing it, and forcing risks the digivolution going dark instead.

Roll d100 the moment you digivolve. Roll ABOVE the threshold below and the digivolution holds cleanly, at whatever reduced spend rate you chose. That rate becomes your ongoing Hope cost per turn for as long as your Digimon holds this stage. Roll at or under the threshold and the Digimon dark digivolves instead (see Dark Digivolution, further down this chapter).

**Threshold = (1 - Hope Spent / Full Cost) x Stage Danger**

Stage Danger values: Rookie 20, Champion 35, Ultimate 55, Mega 80.

> **Worth Knowing: Dark Digivolving Chance by Stage**
>
> That formula is easiest to read as a straight percentage chance of going dark, since a d100 roll at or under the threshold triggers it:
>
> | Hope Spent (% of Full Cost) | Rookie | Champion | Ultimate | Mega |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 100% (full cost) | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
> | 75% | 5% | 9% | 14% | 20% |
> | 50% | 10% | 18% | 28% | 40% |
> | 25% | 15% | 26% | 41% | 60% |
> | 0% (free) | 20% | 35% | 55% | 80% |
>
> Paying nothing at all is never a guaranteed dark digivolution at any stage, but it gets a lot less safe the higher the stage climbs. Sustaining a Rookie for free only risks going dark 1 time in 5. Doing the same at Mega fails more often than it works.

Out of combat you must always pay the full Hope cost for whatever stage you're holding. Forcing, spending less than full and risking a dark digivolution, is only ever available in combat.

## **HP on Digivolution**

| **Action** | **HP Rule** |
| --- | --- |
| Digivolving up | Current HP increases by the difference between old and new max HP. |
| Dedigivolving | Current HP is capped at the new lower max HP. |
| Out-of-combat healing | Valid strategy: digivolve up then dedigivolve to bank HP gain. Must pay full Hope cost. No forcing out of combat. |

## **Hitting 0 HP: The Cascade**

| **Situation** | **What Happens** |
| --- | --- |
| Above Default Stage | Revert to Default Stage at 1 HP. Still in the fight. |
| At Default Stage | Default Stage drops by 1. Remain at 1 HP. |
| Overkill hit (>50% max HP) | Default Stage drops by 1 additionally. Stacks with the above. |
| Egg state | If Default Stage cannot drop further, become an egg. Out until next session. |

## **Dark Digivolution**

When a forced digivolution roll fails, the Digimon doesn't just fail to hold its stage; it goes dark instead. The GM chooses which dark form actually emerges.

A dark form costs nothing to maintain. It holds itself with no Hope spent per turn at all, and it doesn't revert early no matter what happens, staying dark until the encounter itself ends. While dark, the GM controls the Digimon directly: it attacks whoever is closest, Tamer or Digimon alike, and favors targets it holds a type advantage against. The dark form's signature move stays with the Digimon for good once the encounter ends, permanently added to its Move Pool alongside everything else it's unlocked. The dark state itself only ends when the encounter does, whether that's every enemy defeated, the dark Digimon defeated, or everyone else choosing to submit.

> **Submitting to a Dark Digimon**
>
> Facing a dark Digimon, a Tamer can choose to submit rather than keep fighting it. Submitting pulls both the Tamer and their partner out of the encounter entirely; neither one can be targeted by the dark Digimon anymore. The cost is steep: the moment a Tamer submits, their Hope drops straight to 0.
>
> If the dark Digimon a Tamer is submitting to is their own partner, only the Tamer leaves the encounter this way. Their Digimon stays in the fight, still under the GM's control until the encounter ends, since it isn't answering to its Tamer anymore anyway.

## **Daily Rest Recovery**

After each full day of rest, if the Digimon's Default Stage is below their Max Default Stage, it increases by one. Default Stage cannot exceed Max Default Stage through any means.
